About

I started in the engine room (Linux systems, virtualisation, infrastructure) and moved to the boardroom conversation about what technology actually costs and whether it's worth it.

That journey gave me something rare: I speak both languages. I understand how engineers think about architecture decisions, and I understand how CFOs think about returns. Technology economics is precisely that intersection.

At AVIV Group, I lead cloud financial management across Germany, Belgium and France: a chargeback model that puts cloud spend in the hands of the P&L owners who generate it, unit economics that tie spend to business drivers, and more than $1M a year in sustained, tracked savings.

Where I'm investing now: AI economics. GPU capacity, token consumption and inference are the fastest-growing and least governed lines in most technology budgets. The same primitives that made cloud spend accountable (allocation, unit economics, guardrails) are what will make AI spend accountable. AVIV was an early customer of the AWS FinOps Agent, and I'm building that layer now.

Philosophy

"Cloud spend isn't a cost to minimise. It's an investment to govern — and that's a very different conversation."

Bridging languages

Technology and Finance speak different dialects. The most valuable thing I do is translate — so both sides can make better decisions together.

Governance over restriction

The goal isn't to stop teams from spending. It's to ensure every euro of cloud investment has accountability, visibility, and a clear business rationale.

Pragmatic over perfect

Frameworks that live in spreadsheets don't change behaviour. I build systems that people actually use — because they make their work easier, not harder.
